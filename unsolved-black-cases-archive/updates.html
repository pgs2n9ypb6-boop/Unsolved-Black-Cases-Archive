Corrections & Updates

UNSOLVED BLACK CASES ARCHIVE corrects case files when new sourcing, official records, or documented errors come to light. This page is a dated, public record of substantive corrections and additions — not routine copyediting, but changes that affect what a reader would understand about a case: a corrected date, a status update, a name added or removed, a source verified. Nothing is corrected quietly.

See How We Research for our sourcing standards, or Submit a Tip to flag something that needs review.

2026-08-25

  • Case #041 — Lester Mitchell: New case added to the archive: Lester Mitchell (Dayton, Ohio, 1966), sourced from the Dayton Daily News and BlackPast.org. This is the first Ohio case documented in the archive.
  • Case #042 — Timothy Thomas: New case added to the archive: Timothy Thomas (Cincinnati, Ohio, 2001), sourced from The Washington Post and the IAED Journal's case retrospective.
  • Case #039 — Matthew Williams: New case added to the archive: Matthew Williams (Salisbury, Maryland, 1931), sourced from the Maryland State Archives and the International Center for Transitional Justice. This is the first Maryland case documented in the archive.
  • Case #040 — Carl Hampton: New case added to the archive: Carl Hampton (Houston, Texas, 1970), sourced from the Houston Chronicle and Liberation News.
  • Case #037 — James Powell: New case added to the archive: James Powell (Manhattan, New York, 1964), sourced from extensive contemporaneous and historical reporting on the Harlem riot of 1964. This is the first New York case documented in the archive.
  • Case #038 — Rogers Hamilton: New case added to the archive: Rogers Hamilton (Hayneville, Alabama, 1957), sourced from the DOJ's 2016 Notice to Close File and Alabama Reporter's 2025 investigative follow-up.

This is the first Tennessee case documented in the archive, and the earliest case tied to formal NAACP civil rights organizing.
  • Case #032 — Henry "Dickie" Marrow Jr.: New case added to the archive: Henry “Dickie” Marrow Jr. (Oxford, North Carolina, 1970), sourced from the DOJ's Civil Rights Division case file and the North Carolina Department of Natural and Cultural Resources. This is the first South Carolina case documented in the archive.

2026-08-22

  • Case #007 — Harry T. Moore & Harriette Moore: Added a verified, public-domain photograph of Harry T. Moore, sourced directly from the State Archives of Florida (Florida Memory). No verified photograph of Harriette Moore alone has been located yet.

2026-08-21

  • Case #002 — Darlenia Denise Johnson: Corrected the location of her summer job, previously conflated with her home neighborhood. She lived in Congress Heights; she was on her way to work at the Oxon Hill Recreation Center, a separate location, when she was abducted.
  • Case #003 — Brenda Faye Crockett: Added her exact disappearance date, July 27, 1971, previously recorded only as “July 1971.”
  • Case #005 — Brenda Denise Woodard: Added her exact abduction date and circumstances — the night of November 15, 1971, after leaving night school — which were not previously on record.
  • Case #007 — Harry T. Moore & Harriette Moore: Added that the FBI reviewed the case again in 2008 under the Emmett Till Unsolved Civil Rights Crime Act, and that the DOJ formally closed the file in 2011.
  • Case #009 — Hattie DeBardelaben: Added the names of the three federal officers present at the search, previously described only generically as “three federal officers.” Also added that an undertaker reportedly found signs consistent with a broken neck, contradicting the officially recorded cause of death (heart attack).
  • Case #011 — Alberta Jones: Corrected case status. This file previously stated the DOJ's cold-case review “remains open”; the DOJ in fact formally closed the file in August 2023 without identifying a suspect. Updated the summary accordingly.
  • Case #012 — Alonzo Brooks: Corrected status from Cold Case to Unsolved to reflect the active federal hate-crime investigation. Added the exact body-recovery date, May 1, 2004, previously recorded only as “about a month later.” Added a verified FBI photograph.
  • Case #013 — Diamond and Tionda Bradley: Added verified FBI photographs of both sisters, and the FBI's specific published reward amount ($10,000), previously described only as “a reward.”
