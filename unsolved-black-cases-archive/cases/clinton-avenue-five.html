CASE FILE #134

The Clinton Avenue Five

Summary Profile Sources Unanswered Questions
Victim

The Clinton Avenue Five

TF
No verified photo on file

1978 · Newark, NJ

Last Known Information

Melvin Pittman, 17, Ernest Taylor, 17, Alvin Turner, 16, Randy Johnson, 16, and Michael McDowell, 16 — five teenagers known as the Clinton Avenue Five — vanished together in Newark, New Jersey on August 20, 1978, after playing basketball and getting into a pickup truck driven by Lee Anthony Evans, a local handyman who paid them in odd jobs. The case went cold as a missing-persons file for three decades until 2008, when Evans's cousin Philander Hampton, in police custody on an unrelated charge, confessed after a 13-hour interrogation that Evans had lured the boys to a vacant house, locked them in a closet at gunpoint over a stolen-marijuana dispute, and set the building on fire, killing all five. Hampton pleaded guilty and received a 10-year sentence plus $15,000 in relocation money in exchange for testifying against Evans. At Evans's 2011 trial, where he represented himself, a jury acquitted him of all ten murder-related charges, finding the prosecution's case — built almost entirely on Hampton's confession, with no bodies or physical evidence ever recovered — insufficient. No one has ever been convicted of killing the five boys, whose remains have never been found.

Location

Newark, NJ · Essex County

Investigation
  • The five teenagers' identities, ages, and the August 20, 1978 disappearance after boarding Evans's pickup truck, per Wikipedia's sourced case history and contemporaneous reporting.
  • In November 2008, Philander Hampton confessed after a 13-hour police interrogation that he and his cousin, Lee Anthony Evans, lured the boys to a vacant house at 256 Camden Street, locked them in a third-floor closet at gunpoint, and burned the building down with them inside.
  • Sonar searches of the fire site, conducted after Hampton's confession, found no human remains.
  • Evans and Hampton were arrested and charged with felony murder and arson in March 2010.
  • Hampton pleaded guilty and received a 10-year prison sentence and $15,000 in relocation money in exchange for testifying against Evans.
  • A jury acquitted Evans, who represented himself at trial, of all ten murder-related charges in November 2011, finding the prosecution had not proven its case beyond a reasonable doubt.
  • Evans later filed a federal civil suit against the Essex County prosecutor's office and Newark police alleging his confession-based prosecution relied on coerced testimony and fabricated evidence; a federal appeals panel ruled in 2025 that the detectives involved were not entitled to qualified immunity from that suit.
What Remains Unknown
  • Whether Evans was in fact responsible for the boys' deaths, as Hampton's confession described, has never been established beyond that single confession — the jury found it insufficient, and no physical evidence or remains have ever corroborated it.
  • The specific details of what led two Newark detectives to reopen the case in 2008 after three decades, and what led to Hampton's 13-hour interrogation, have been disputed in Evans's later civil litigation.
  • The extent to which coercion may have shaped Hampton's confession, as alleged in Evans's civil suit, remains an open legal question as of the 2025 appellate ruling.
